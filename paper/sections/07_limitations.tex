\section{Limitations and Future Work}

\paragraph{Unknown contamination count.}
The main theorem is stated first for an exact count \(m\), but Corollary~1 extends anytime validity to the practically weaker assumption \(m^\star\le M\) by taking a lower envelope across candidate sizes and using the size-specific predictive null law. The frozen experiments nevertheless use the exact count \(m=2\). The empirical power and computational cost of the upper-bound-only procedure remain to be studied.

\paragraph{Static/exogenous contamination.}
The theorem requires that deleting the true contaminated cells leaves an iid clean reference sample. It does not cover value-adaptive replacement chosen after inspecting clean realized values. Such selection can bias the surviving clean subset.

\paragraph{Scalar continuous observations.}
The theorem assumes scalar observations from a continuous null. Quantized, discrete, vector, complex-valued, and tied data require additional construction, such as randomized ranks or application-specific scalar scores.

\paragraph{Independence and stationarity.}
Clean references and future null observations are iid from the same unknown \(F\). Temporal dependence, drift, and reference/test mismatch are outside the current theorem and are particularly relevant in RF sensing.

\paragraph{Contamination-geometry scope.}
The pre-specified V1b stress test expands beyond the original two contaminants at value \(2\) to upper in-support, mixed low/high, and independently random in-support static contamination. The empirical advantage survives those four geometries, but this finite matrix does not imply uniform robustness over all possible static contamination values or mechanisms.

\paragraph{Finite-reference asymptotics.}
The asymptotic proposition shows that the frozen finite portfolio is not universally wealth-divergent for fixed reference size: unmatched fixed categorical components decay exponentially, while Dirichlet predictive components converge to finite prior-density ratios under interior limiting rank frequencies. This characterizes the present bettor family rather than all possible predictable betting strategies.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo scope.}
The publication-scale and V1b validations use 5,000 replications per condition and report Wilson or paired-bootstrap 95\% intervals. These quantify Monte Carlo uncertainty under the frozen data-generating families, not uncertainty over alternative physical environments.

\paragraph{Baseline scope.}
The contaminated-reference confidence-band comparators are our adaptations of clean-reference DKW/CCTM ideas. They are neither canonical nor optimal contaminated-CCTM procedures. The best-grid envelope is descriptive and ex post; V1b's primary \texttt{band\_mix}, \(\delta=0.045\), comparator is fixed before outcomes.

\paragraph{No optimality claim.}
The lower envelope is valid, but no minimax or uniformly most powerful property is proved. Better betting families or candidate aggregation strategies may exist.

\paragraph{Full-mixture optimization.}
The polynomial dynamic program applies only to a fixed categorical bettor. The complete frozen portfolio is evaluated by enumeration. Scalable exact or certified approximate optimization is future work; any approximation used for testing must preserve a conservative lower bound compatible with Theorem~1.

\paragraph{RTL-SDR validation scope.}
The semi-synthetic V1/V2 experiments use real RTL-SDR receiver/background IQ and controlled post-ADC injection. They therefore preserve receiver quantization and environmental structure but do not test signal-induced analog front-end nonlinearities. The score streams also exhibit substantial serial dependence, so hardware-null crossing frequencies are descriptive and do not validate the iid theorem or establish hardware type-I control. Additional SDR acquisition is not required for the present paper; a future over-the-air or dependence-aware study would address a different inferential problem.

\paragraph{Persistent-identity efficiency.}
The experiments indicate that enforcing one persistent latent contamination explanation can retain much more evidence than the tested generic CDF-band robustifications. A quantitative theorem characterizing when and by how much persistence improves finite-horizon detection remains open. Any such result should be treated as a separate theoretical extension rather than inferred from the current experiments.
