\section{Results}

\subsection{Null sanity check}

The publication-scale null experiment uses 5,000 paths per \(K\). At \(T=200\), observed crossing probabilities for the proposed method are \(0.0112\) (95\% CI \([0.0086,0.0145]\)) for \(K=24\) and \(0.0144\) (95\% CI \([0.0115,0.0181]\)) for \(K=32\). Oracle null crossing probabilities are \(0.0328\) and \(0.0324\), respectively. The strongest frozen-grid confidence-band benchmark has zero crossings in both null settings. These Monte Carlo estimates do not establish validity; they provide an implementation sanity check against the nominal anytime level \(\alpha=0.05\).

\subsection{Semi-synthetic RTL-SDR validation}

SDR V1 uses 20 fresh captures. At \(-12\) dB, static coupling and the oracle cross on 20/20 captures, while the fixed band comparator crosses on 15/20; median detected stopping times are 4.5, 3.5, and 29, respectively. The stronger V1 SNR values saturate in crossing probability.

SDR V2 therefore uses 30 fresh captures and a weaker grid frozen before V2 detector outcomes. At \(-18\) dB, static coupling crosses on 14/30 captures, the fixed band comparator on 0/30, and the oracle on 18/30. At \(-15\) dB, the counts are 22/30, 12/30, and 24/30, with median detected stopping times 8, 58.5, and 5. At the shared \(-12\) dB anchor, static coupling crosses on 30/30 captures in V2, with median stop 4, compared with 26/30 and median stop 30 for the baseline.

\subsection{Interpretation}

A confidence-band method asks which null CDFs remain compatible with the contaminated reference. Static coupling asks which \emph{single persistent set of contaminated identities} could explain the entire repeated rank history. The simulation, geometry stress test, and semi-synthetic receiver experiments consistently support the practical value of retaining this persistent combinatorial constraint, while the theorem remains limited to the stated iid static/exogenous model.
