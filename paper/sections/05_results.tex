\section{Results}

\subsection{Null sanity check}

At \(T=200\), observed null crossing probabilities for the proposed method are \(0.020\) for \(K=24\) and \(0.015\) for \(K=32\). Oracle null crossing probabilities are \(0.060\) and \(0.040\), respectively. These Monte Carlo estimates do not establish validity; they only provide an implementation sanity check against the nominal level \(\alpha=0.05\).

At the pre-specified primary point \(K=32,\gamma=4,T=200\),
\[
  \widehat P_{\mathrm{static}}=0.935,\qquad

  \widehat P_{\mathrm{oracle}}=0.995.
\]
The primary gap is therefore
\[
  0.935-0.170=0.765,
\]
well above the frozen strong-pass margin of 0.15. Relative to the oracle, the static method retains approximately \(0.935/0.995\approx0.94\) of the observed crossing probability in this scenario. This ratio is descriptive, not an efficiency theorem.

For the strong \(\gamma=8\) alternative at \(K=32\), both static coupling and the oracle cross on every simulated path, while the strongest confidence-band comparator reaches \(0.840\). The smaller absolute gap is consistent with saturation under a strong signal.

For the weak \(\gamma=2\) alternative, static crossing probability is only \(0.225\) at \(K=24\) and \(0.195\) at \(K=32\), while the oracle reaches \(0.495\) and \(0.425\). Unknown contamination identities therefore impose a substantial finite-reference penalty under weak shifts.

\subsection{Evidence diagnostics and tuning}

At \(K=32,\gamma=4,T=200\), median terminal log evidence is \(9.309764\) for static coupling and \(13.358775\) for the oracle, compared with \(-1.402095\) for the strongest band mixture, \(-1.601856\) for band ONS, and \(-2.160841\) for the CCTM-style comparator.

At \(K=32,\gamma=8,T=200\), corresponding medians are \(27.937381\), \(33.873675\), \(18.750086\), \(19.274469\), and \(5.313818\). These values are diagnostic only because the confidence-band thresholds differ from 20.

At \(K=32,\gamma=4,T=200\), the strongest band mixture uses \(\delta=0.040\), crossing probability \(0.170\), and threshold 96. Band ONS reaches \(0.160\) with \(\delta=0.045\) and threshold 191. The CCTM-style comparator reaches \(0.045\) at \(\delta=0.040,D=0.99\). At \(K=32,\gamma=8,T=200\), the strongest band mixture reaches \(0.840\) at \(\delta=0.045\).

An earlier frozen comparison with \(\delta=0.025\) already showed non-vacuous confidence-band power: at \(K=32,T=200\), the exact-count band reaches \(0.120\) for \(\gamma=4\) and \(0.735\) for \(\gamma=8\), while the CCTM-style comparator reaches \(0.010\) and \(0.300\), respectively. Thus the final advantage is not merely an artifact of a mathematically vacuous band.

\subsection{Interpretation}

A confidence-band method asks which null CDFs remain compatible with the contaminated reference. Static coupling asks which \emph{single persistent set of contaminated identities} could explain the entire repeated rank history. The experiments suggest that preserving this persistent combinatorial constraint can retain substantially more usable finite-reference evidence, especially in the moderate-shift regime.
